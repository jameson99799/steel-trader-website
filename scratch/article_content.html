What is Galvalume Steel? Understanding the 55% Al-Zn Revolution

Galvalume — also known as GL, Aluzinc, or Zincalume® — represents one of the most significant innovations in steel coating technology since its development by Bethlehem Steel Corporation in 1972. Unlike traditional galvanized steel that uses pure zinc coating, Galvalume employs a unique alloy of 55% aluminum, 43.4% zinc, and 1.6% silicon applied through continuous hot-dip coating.

This aluminum-zinc alloy creates a dual-phase microstructure: an aluminum-rich dendritic matrix surrounded by zinc-rich inter-dendritic channels. This structure combines aluminum's excellent barrier protection with zinc's sacrificial corrosion protection, delivering performance that exceeds either metal alone.

How Does Galvalume Resist Corrosion? The Science Behind Al-Zn Coating

Galvalume's superior corrosion resistance comes from a three-layer protection mechanism:

  1. Aluminum Oxide Barrier Layer — The aluminum component forms a stable, self-healing Al₂O₃ oxide film that acts as a physical barrier against moisture and atmospheric pollutants. This barrier is responsible for the exceptional flat-panel corrosion life.
  2. Zinc Sacrificial Protection — The zinc-rich areas provide cathodic (sacrificial) protection at cut edges and scratches, similar to galvanized steel but with a more controlled dissolution rate that extends service life.
  3. Silicon Bonding Agent — The 1.6% silicon content ensures metallurgical bonding between the Al-Zn alloy and the steel substrate, preventing delamination even under severe forming conditions.

Research by the International Zinc Association (IZA) and field studies spanning over 40 years have consistently demonstrated that Galvalume coated panels provide 2 to 6 times longer service life than equivalent galvanized panels in identical exposure conditions.

Key Applications of Galvalume Steel

1. Metal Roofing & Wall Cladding

GL steel accounts for over 70% of the global metal roofing market. Its combination of corrosion resistance, heat reflectivity, and low maintenance makes it the preferred choice for:

  • Residential roofing (standing seam, corrugated, tile-effect profiles)
  • Commercial and industrial roofing (long-span, through-fastened, concealed-fix)
  • Architectural wall cladding (flat panels, cassettes, composite systems)

2. Solar Energy Infrastructure

The 25+ year durability of GL steel perfectly matches solar panel warranties, making it the preferred substrate for panel mounting frames, tracker systems, and carport structures.

3. Automotive Heat Management

GL's heat resistance to 315°C makes it essential for exhaust heat shields, catalytic converter covers, and underbody thermal protection in modern vehicles.

Galvalume vs Galvanized Steel: Making the Right Choice

While both GL and GI serve similar markets, they have distinct strengths:

  • Choose Galvalume (GL) when flat-panel corrosion resistance is paramount — roofing, cladding, solar structures, and exposed architectural panels.
  • Choose Galvanized (GI) when heavy forming, welding, or cut-edge protection is needed — structural steel, tubing, post-forming applications.
  • Choose PPGL (Prepainted Galvalume) when you need both superior corrosion resistance AND decorative color — the most durable prepainted metal option.

How to Evaluate Your GL Supplier: 5 Critical Checkpoints

  1. Coating Weight Certification — Request original MTC with XRF-measured coating weight per EN 10204 Type 3.1. Reputable mills like FADA provide this standard.
  2. Surface Quality Standards — Specify surface class (A, B, or C per ASTM A792) based on whether the GL will be exposed, painted, or concealed.
  3. Mechanical Properties Testing — Verify yield/tensile/elongation values meet your structural requirements, especially for roll-formed profiles.
  4. Packaging Quality — Proper sea-worthy packing with moisture barriers, interleaving paper, and edge protectors prevents transit damage.
  5. Production Capability — Ensure your supplier has dedicated CGL (Continuous Galvalume Line) with precise alloy bath control, not just a galvanizing line used for both products.
